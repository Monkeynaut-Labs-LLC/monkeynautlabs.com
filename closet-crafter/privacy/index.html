👨‍👩‍👧 CHILDREN'S PRIVACY / COPPA NOTICE — read Section 4 Users under 13 receive a fully on-device version of Closet Crafter: no personal information is collected or transmitted from children. Parents: see Section 4 below and the FTC's COPPA guidance at https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy.

Closet Crafter is built to keep your wardrobe private. There are no accounts, no ads, and no tracking. This policy explains exactly what data exists, where it goes, and what control you have.

1. Where your wardrobe lives

Closet Crafter stores your wardrobe photos, garment details, outfits, favorites, categories, moods, themes, day overrides, what the app has learned about how you use it, and your birthday. We operate no accounts and no database of users — none of this is ever sent to Monkeynaut Labs.

A full copy always lives on your device. In addition, iCloud sync is on by default, which keeps your wardrobe and settings consistent across the devices signed in to your Apple Account. When it is on, your wardrobe data, photos, and app settings are stored in your own private iCloud, under your Apple Account — not on our servers. We cannot see it, and Apple's iCloud terms and your iCloud storage apply. You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Wardrobe Storage → This Device Only, and the app keeps working entirely on-device. When you do, the app asks whether to keep the copy in iCloud or remove it; removing it erases your wardrobe, photos and settings from iCloud (and from other devices that sync with it) the next time you open the app.

Your birthday never leaves your device. It is deliberately excluded from iCloud sync, so it is never uploaded and never travels to your other devices — each device asks for it separately. It is used only to keep the app age-appropriate (see Section 4) and to tailor on-device suggestions.

The camera. Closet Crafter uses the camera to photograph your clothes, and in Mirror View to show an outfit over your reflection. Mirror View finds where your shoulders, hips and ankles are in the camera image, on your device, so the outfit can follow your reflection; those points exist only while the screen is open. It keeps nothing: no frame or body measurement is recorded, stored, or sent anywhere, and no AI sees it. What it does keep is how you nudged each kind of piece (for example "tops a little bigger"), so the next session fits better. The level shown while you take a photo reads the phone's motion sensors on the device only.

Things the app does on your device only. These send nothing anywhere:

  • Reading printed words. Words printed on a garment and its care label are read on your device and saved with that garment.
  • The Today's Outfit widget. The app keeps small pictures and names of the pieces in your last outfit on your device so the widget can show them. The widget does not use the network.
  • Siri and Shortcuts. "Craft an outfit" only opens the app. Closet Crafter does not receive or keep what you say to Siri.
  • Visual Intelligence. When you ask your iPhone what you own that looks like something in front of the camera, Apple's system passes Closet Crafter that image and the match against your wardrobe is made on your device. The image is not kept.
  • Clean Up. When you paint over part of a garment photo in Edit Photo, the area is filled in by a model that runs on your device. The photo never leaves your device. The model itself is downloaded once, the first time you use Clean Up, from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), which, like any download, sees your device's internet address. Nothing about you or your photos is sent with it.
  • Holidays. Upcoming US holidays come from a calendar built into the app. Their names, which are public information, can be included in an AI request.

2. Data that leaves your device to third parties — only when you use an AI feature

  • AI analysis and styling (optional): Closet Crafter offers up to three ways to run its AI, and you choose which:
    • Apple Intelligence on your device. Nothing leaves your device.
    • Apple Private Cloud Compute, where your device supports it. Wardrobe photos (downscaled), your styling prompts, mood labels, and a weather summary are sent to Apple for processing. Apple states that it keeps nothing and cannot read the request.
    • Closet Crafter Pro. The same content is sent to our server, which forwards it to an AI model provider (currently OpenAI) using our own account and returns the result. Our server does not store your photos or prompts. The provider handles that content under its own privacy terms — we do not control its retention. Our server runs on Cloudflare, which, like any web service, sees your device's internet address when the app connects. The app tells you this before you turn Pro on, and Pro is never turned on for you.
    • What is never sent with any of these: your birthday or anything worked out from it, your name, your email, or your location.
  • Closet Crafter Pro (subscription): your subscription status is managed by Apple and RevenueCat (our subscription processor) under a random anonymous identifier — never your name or email. Our server keeps only transient usage counters against that identifier to enforce fair-use limits; they expire automatically. During the free TestFlight beta there is no subscription: the app makes up a random identifier on your device instead, used only for those same usage counters.
  • Weather: with your permission, your location is sent to Apple's WeatherKit to fetch local weather. We never see or store it.
  • Payments: all payment processing is by Apple. We never see payment details.

Styling preferences: Closet Crafter learns your taste on your device from the outfits you save, the individual pieces you swap out, the suggestions you pass on, and your thumbs-up/down on styling notes. Only a short derived summary (for example, "usually casual; often navy") is ever included in AI requests to personalize suggestions. The underlying history is never sent to us or to any AI provider; with iCloud sync on it is stored in your own private iCloud so it follows you between your devices, and Delete All Data erases it from both.

If you never enable an AI feature, nothing is sent to any third party. iCloud sync, when on, is between you and Apple.

People in photos: Closet Crafter performs no facial recognition and never attempts to identify anyone; AI analysis is instructed to describe clothing only and to ignore people. However, if a photo includes a person, that image is still transmitted to the provider when an AI feature runs — please avoid including other people in your photos without their permission.

3. What we never collect

Name, email, phone number, contacts, account credentials, precise identity of any kind, analytics profiles, or advertising identifiers. We do not track you across apps or websites and we do not sell data.

4. Children's privacy (COPPA)

Closet Crafter's online AI features require a verified age of 13 or older, collected as a neutral birthday step during setup. Users under 13 (and anyone who has not completed the age step) automatically receive the fully on-device version: no photos, prompts, or any other data are transmitted to Monkeynaut Labs or to any AI provider, and the birthday itself never leaves the device.

iCloud sync is the one exception to "nothing is transmitted", and it is worth being precise about: if iCloud sync is on, the wardrobe is copied to the user's own private iCloud under their own Apple Account. That is a transfer to Apple under the account holder's control — for a child this is normally a Family Sharing child account managed by a parent — not a collection of data by us. We receive nothing either way. Parents who prefer no off-device copy at all can set Settings → Wardrobe Storage → This Device Only.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Because the under-13 experience transmits nothing, no parental consent is required to use it. Parents and guardians: you can review or erase everything the app stores via Settings → Delete All Data on the device, and you can contact us with any question. If you believe a child under 13 has provided personal information through an online feature (for example, by entering an inaccurate birthday), contact us and we will delete it. If an under-13 birthday is entered on a device, online features remain locked on that device even if the birthday is later changed or removed; the only reset is erasing the app's data (Settings → Delete All Data). FTC COPPA guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy.

5. Your controls

  • Delete All Data (Settings) erases everything Closet Crafter stores — photos, wardrobe, settings, day overrides, styling history, what the app has learned, and your birthday — and restarts setup. If iCloud sync is on, this also erases the iCloud copy, which removes it from your other devices too; the app tells you so before you confirm. Your subscription belongs to your Apple Account and is unaffected (use Restore Purchases).
  • Wardrobe Storage (Settings) switches between *This Device Only* and *Device + iCloud*, so you can decline off-device storage entirely.
  • Remove birthday data (Settings) deletes it and re-locks online AI.
  • What the App Has Learned (Settings) shows what the app has picked up from how you use it, and can reset it.
  • Content already processed by an AI provider is governed by that provider's policy and deletion tools; Apple's purchase records are governed by Apple.

6. Security

All network connections use HTTPS. The app holds no AI credentials of any kind. Our Pro service holds AI credentials server-side so they never exist in the app, verifies subscriptions before serving requests, and enforces per-user usage limits.

7. TestFlight beta testing

If you test Closet Crafter through Apple's TestFlight, Apple shares with us what TestFlight collects: the feedback and screenshots you choose to send, crash reports, and basic device information (model, iOS version). If you were invited by email, Apple also shows us that email address and the name on the invitation. We use this only to fix and improve the app, and we do not share it. Apple's TestFlight terms and privacy policy apply to TestFlight itself.

8. Changes & contact

We will update this policy when the app's data practices change and note the effective date above.
